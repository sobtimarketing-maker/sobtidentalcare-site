A lot of people come in asking for a "zirconium cap" because a relative had one, or because the last dentist mentioned it. Most of them aren't quite sure what it is. That's fair. Nobody explains it properly, and the word sounds more like a metal than a ceramic.

So here is what we tell patients in the chair.

## It's a ceramic, not a metal

Zirconia is a white ceramic, and a very tough one. A zirconia crown has no metal inside at all. That matters because of what used to be the standard cap in India: a metal shell with porcelain baked on top. Those caps look fine at first. After a few years the gum tends to pull back slightly, and the dark metal edge starts to show as a grey line. If you've seen that on someone's front tooth, that's why.

With zirconia there's nothing dark underneath, so there's nothing to show.

## Two kinds, for two different jobs

There's more than one kind of zirconia crown, and this is where people get confused.

- Full (monolithic) zirconia is milled from a single solid block. It's the strongest choice. We use it for molars, for people who grind their teeth at night, and for crowns on implants.
- Layered zirconia has a zirconia base with porcelain built up by hand on top. It looks more like natural enamel, so it suits teeth that show when you smile.

For a single front tooth, an E-max crown can look even more natural. We'll write about that choice separately.

## When a tooth needs a crown at all

We don't put a crown on a tooth just because it has a filling. A crown makes sense when the tooth has lost so much of itself that a filling won't hold, when it has cracked, or after a root canal on a back tooth. Root-canal-treated molars become brittle, and a crown stops them splitting down the middle when you bite on something hard.

## How ours are made

Your tooth is shaped and then scanned with a small camera. There are no trays of impression paste. The crown is designed on screen around your bite and milled from a zirconia disc in our lab on the same floor. Then it goes into a furnace, where it hardens and shrinks to its final size, and is stained and glazed to match your other teeth.

Because the lab is down the corridor, the person who made your crown can look at it in your mouth at the try-in. If the shade is a touch off, it's corrected that day. You can read more on our [zirconia crowns page](/zirconia-crowns-delhi.html).

## The part people forget

A zirconia crown can't decay. The tooth under it can, right at the edge where crown meets tooth. So the crown lasts as long as you keep that edge clean: brushing twice a day, cleaning between the teeth, and coming in for check-ups.

## Questions

**Is a zirconia crown the same as a zirconium crown?**
Yes. Zirconium crown, zirconium cap and zirconia crown all mean the same thing.

**Can a zirconia crown be whitened later?**
No. Whitening only works on natural teeth. If you're planning to whiten, do it first, and we'll match the crown to the new shade.
