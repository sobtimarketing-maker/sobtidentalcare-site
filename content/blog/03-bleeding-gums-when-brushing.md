Most people notice it the same way. You spit after brushing and there's a streak of pink in the sink. It doesn't hurt, so it's easy to shrug off. Plenty of our patients tell us their gums have bled "for years" before they mention it.

It's common, but healthy gums don't bleed when you brush. So it's worth knowing what it's telling you.

## The usual reason: plaque along the gum line

In most cases bleeding gums come down to plaque, the soft, sticky film that builds up on teeth every day. When it sits along the gum line, the gums get irritated and swollen. This early stage is called gingivitis. Swollen gums have more blood close to the surface, so they bleed at the slightest touch of a brush or floss.

If plaque is left long enough it hardens into tartar. Tartar can't be brushed off, so the gums stay irritated until it's removed at the clinic.

The good news is that gingivitis usually reverses once the area is properly clean.

## When it goes further

If the irritation carries on for months or years, it can start to affect the bone and the attachment that hold the teeth in place. The gums begin to pull away from the teeth and small pockets form between them, where plaque collects out of reach of a brush. This is gum disease, or periodontitis.

Signs that things have moved past the early stage:

- gums that look like they've shrunk, so teeth look longer
- bad breath or a bad taste that keeps coming back
- teeth that feel slightly loose or seem to have shifted
- gaps opening up between teeth that weren't there before

Any of these is a reason to come in sooner rather than later.

## Other things that can make gums bleed

Plaque is the cause most of the time, but not always. Gums can bleed more during pregnancy, with some medicines that thin the blood, with uncontrolled diabetes, or after brushing too hard with a hard brush. If your gums bleed a lot, bleed without brushing, or you bruise easily as well, tell us, and see your physician too.

## What helps at home

- Keep brushing, even if it bleeds. Gently, twice a day, with a soft brush angled towards the gum line.
- Clean between the teeth once a day with floss or small interdental brushes. Expect some bleeding for the first week. It should ease as the gums settle.
- Don't switch to brushing less. Avoiding the area lets plaque build up and makes the bleeding worse.

If it's still bleeding after a couple of weeks of good cleaning, there's probably tartar there that only a cleaning will remove.

## Questions

**Is it normal for gums to bleed when brushing?**
It's common, but not normal. Healthy gums don't bleed when you brush. Bleeding usually means the gums are inflamed from plaque along the gum line.

**Should I stop brushing if my gums bleed?**
No. Keep brushing gently with a soft brush and clean between your teeth. Brushing less lets more plaque build up and makes the bleeding worse.

**Can bleeding gums go back to normal?**
Early gum inflammation (gingivitis) usually settles once plaque and tartar are removed and you keep the area clean. Gum disease that has affected the bone needs treatment to stop it getting worse.

**When should I see a dentist about bleeding gums?**
If the bleeding hasn't stopped after two weeks of good brushing and flossing, or if your gums have shrunk, your breath stays bad or a tooth feels loose.
