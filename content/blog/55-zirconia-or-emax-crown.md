Once people know they want a metal-free crown, the next question is usually zirconia or E-max. Both are good materials. Both are made in our lab. They're just good at different things.

## The one-line answer

Zirconia is the stronger of the two. E-max looks more like a natural tooth. So, roughly: zirconia for back teeth, E-max for front teeth. But there are exceptions both ways.

## Zirconia

Zirconia is a very tough ceramic. Full zirconia, milled from one solid block, copes with heavy chewing forces, which is why we use it for molars, for people who grind, and on implants. It's also forgiving when the tooth underneath is dark, because it hides colour well.

Its weakness, if you can call it that, is that it's more opaque. On a front tooth, next to natural teeth, plain zirconia can look a little flat. Layered zirconia, with porcelain built up on top by hand, gets around most of that.

## E-max

E-max is a glass ceramic. It lets light pass into it and bounce back the way enamel does, so it has a depth that's hard to match. For a single front tooth, especially next to untouched natural teeth, it's often our first choice.

It's strong enough for most front teeth and many premolars, but it isn't the material we'd pick for someone who grinds heavily on their back teeth. E-max crowns can also often be milled and fitted the same day in our lab.

## When the usual rule changes

- A dark front tooth, for example after an old root canal, may need zirconia or a layered crown to hide the colour.
- A heavy grinder may be better off with zirconia even on a premolar that shows a little.
- Several front teeth done together can look great in either, because there's no natural neighbour to compare against.

## Questions

**Which lasts longer, zirconia or E-max?**
In the right place, both last many years. Zirconia has more margin for heavy biting, which is why it's the safer choice on molars.

**Can I have a zirconia crown on a back tooth and E-max on a front tooth?**
Yes, and that's very common. We match the shades so they look like one smile.

**Can E-max really be done in one day?**
Often, yes, because we mill it in our own lab. It depends on the tooth, so we'll confirm at your check-up.
