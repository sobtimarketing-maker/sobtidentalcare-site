TITLE: Does scaling damage your teeth?
DATE: 2026-12-04
DESC: Scaling doesn't thin enamel or loosen healthy teeth. Why teeth can feel sensitive or gappy after a cleaning, and what's really going on.
AUTHOR: Dr Sobti's Dental Care
---
We hear this one at the desk every week. "My mother says scaling makes the teeth weak." Or "After my last cleaning there were gaps between my teeth." People put off cleanings for years because of it.

So let's go through what scaling actually does.

## What scaling removes

Plaque is the soft, sticky film that builds up on teeth every day. If it isn't brushed off, it hardens into tartar, a chalky deposit that sits along the gum line and behind the lower front teeth. Brushing can't move tartar once it's set.

Scaling takes that tartar off. We use an ultrasonic scaler, which vibrates and sprays water to break the deposit away, and fine hand instruments for the edges. The tip works on the tartar, not on the enamel. It doesn't thin the teeth.

## "But my teeth felt loose afterwards"

This is the bit that frightens people, and it's worth understanding. When tartar has been there for a long time, the gum underneath gets inflamed and starts to pull away. The tartar fills the space where gum used to be, and can even act like a splint between teeth.

Take the tartar away and you see the real situation underneath: a small gap, or a tooth that was already a little loose because of gum disease. The cleaning didn't make the gap. It uncovered it. The good news is that once the gums are clean they usually tighten up again over the following weeks.

## Why teeth feel sensitive for a day or two

Parts of the root that were covered by tartar are now touching air and cold water for the first time in a while. A bit of sensitivity for a day or two is normal and settles on its own. A sensitive toothpaste helps if it bothers you.

## What actually damages teeth

Leaving tartar in place is what does the harm. It keeps the gums inflamed, the bone around the teeth slowly shrinks, and that is how teeth really do get loose over the years. Regular scaling is one of the simplest ways to stop that.

## How often

For most people, every six months. If you smoke, have gum problems or wear braces, every three or four months is better. ## Questions

**Does scaling hurt?**
Most people feel vibration and water, not pain. If your gums are very sore, we can numb the area first.

**Will scaling make my teeth whiter?**
It removes stains and tartar, so teeth often look brighter. It doesn't change their natural shade. That's whitening, which is a separate treatment.

**My gums bleed during scaling. Is that bad?**
It means the gums were inflamed. The bleeding usually stops within a few days of the cleaning as the gums heal.
