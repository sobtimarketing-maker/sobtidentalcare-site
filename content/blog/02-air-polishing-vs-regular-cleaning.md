More and more people now call us and ask for air polishing by name. Usually a friend had it done, or they saw a video of the spray. And the next question is almost always the same: is it better than a normal cleaning, or is it just a fancier polish?

The honest answer is that they do different jobs, and most mouths need a bit of both.

## What a regular cleaning does

A regular cleaning has two parts. Scaling comes first. It removes tartar, the hard, chalky deposit that builds up along the gum line and between the lower front teeth. Once plaque hardens into tartar, brushing won't shift it, and it keeps the gums irritated until it's taken off.

Then comes polishing, traditionally with a small rubber cup and a gritty paste. That smooths the surfaces so fresh plaque has less to hold on to.

## What air polishing does differently

Air polishing replaces the rubber cup. A small handpiece sprays a fine powder in a jet of air and water, and that spray lifts stains and soft plaque off the teeth.

The difference is mostly about reach. A cup can only polish the surfaces it touches. A spray gets into the places a cup struggles with: between teeth, along the edge of the gum, around braces, and around crowns and implants. It also works well on the stains people mind most, from tea, coffee, paan and smoking.

There's no gritty paste and no scraping, so most people find it gentler than they expected. It needs its own machine, which is why not every clinic offers it.

## Why we often do both

Air polishing is very good at stains and soft plaque, but it doesn't remove hard tartar. So if there's tartar, scaling still comes first, and air polishing finishes the job. If your teeth are already fairly clean and you mainly want stains gone, air polishing on its own may be enough. We'll look first and tell you which you need.

## What it won't do

Air polishing isn't bleaching. It brings teeth back to their own natural shade, and for many people that's already a noticeable change. If you'd like them lighter than their natural colour, that's whitening, which is a separate treatment.

And if your gums bleed easily or have started to pull away from the teeth, a polish of any kind isn't the answer. That may need a deeper clean below the gum line, done a quarter of the mouth at a time.

## How often

Most people do well with a cleaning every six months, along with a check-up. Heavy tea and coffee drinkers, smokers and people wearing braces often come in a bit more often.

## Questions

**Is air polishing better than normal cleaning?**
It reaches between teeth and around braces, crowns and implants better than a polishing cup, and most people find it more comfortable. But it doesn't remove hard tartar, so scaling is still needed when there is tartar.

**Does air polishing remove tartar?**
No. Air polishing removes stains and soft plaque. Hard tartar is removed by scaling, which is why the two are often done in the same visit.

**Does air polishing whiten teeth?**
It removes surface stains, so teeth look brighter, but it doesn't change their natural shade. That needs whitening.
