When a back tooth has been taken out, the question we hear next is nearly always "Should I get a bridge or an implant?" Both give you a fixed tooth that doesn't come out at night. Both can look natural. So how do you choose?

The short answer is that it depends on the teeth on either side of the gap.

## How a bridge works

A bridge is usually three crowns joined into one piece. The two outer crowns fit over the teeth beside the gap, and the middle one is the new tooth. To fit those outer crowns, both neighbouring teeth have to be shaped down.

If those teeth already have big fillings, or already have crowns, that's not much of a loss. They probably needed covering anyway. But if they're healthy, untouched teeth, you're drilling two good teeth to replace one.

## How an implant works

An implant is a small titanium post placed in the bone where the root used to be. After it heals, a crown goes on top. The teeth beside the gap aren't touched at all. An implant also keeps loading the bone, which helps stop the gap from shrinking over the years.

The trade-off is time. An implant needs a minor surgery and usually a few months of healing before the final tooth. A bridge is generally done in about a week.

## How we usually decide

- If the neighbouring teeth are healthy, we'll normally suggest an implant first, and explain why.
- If they're heavily filled or already crowned, a bridge is often the sensible choice.
- If you'd rather avoid surgery, or there isn't enough bone and you don't want grafting, a bridge is a good option.
- For a single front tooth with a suitable bite, a bonded bridge with small wings glued behind the neighbouring teeth needs very little drilling.

None of this is decided over the phone. We look at the teeth, the gums and, for an implant, the bone on a 3D scan taken in the clinic.

## Looking after either one

A bridge needs cleaning underneath the false tooth every day, with a floss threader or a water flosser. An implant needs brushing and flossing like a natural tooth. Both need regular check-ups.

## Questions

**Does a bridge last as long as an implant?**
Both can last many years. Bridges usually fail when one of the supporting teeth decays, which is why cleaning under it matters so much.

**I lost the tooth years ago. Is it too late?**
Usually not. Teeth sometimes drift into an old gap, so we check the space and the bite first.

**Can I get a bridge on implants?**
Yes. When several teeth in a row are missing, a bridge can sit on two or more implants instead of on your own teeth.
