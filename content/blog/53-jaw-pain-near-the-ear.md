Many people with pain just in front of the ear go to an ear doctor first. Sometimes that's the right call. Quite often, though, the ear is fine, and the problem is the joint right next to it: the jaw joint, or TMJ.

## Where the jaw joint is

Put a finger just in front of your ear and open your mouth. The movement you feel is the jaw joint. Because it sits so close to the ear, pain from it is easy to mistake for earache.

## Signs it's coming from the jaw

- an ache in front of the ear or along the jaw, worse when chewing;
- clicking or popping when you open wide;
- a jaw that feels stiff or tired when you wake up;
- headaches at the temples;
- the jaw catching, or not opening as far as it used to.

## The usual cause: clenching and grinding

In most people we see, the joint itself is fine. The muscles that close the jaw are overworked. Many people clench or grind their teeth at night without knowing. A partner might hear it, or you might notice flat, worn edges on your teeth. Stress makes it worse, and so does a bite that has changed after lost back teeth or a filling that sits a little high.

## What helps

Most jaw pain settles without anything drastic.

- Soft food for a week or two, and no gum chewing.
- Warm compresses on the sore side.
- Not opening very wide when you yawn or bite into something big.
- A custom night guard, if you grind or clench. It's a thin appliance made from a scan of your teeth that lets the muscles rest while you sleep. Shop-bought sports guards aren't the same.
- Fixing the bite, such as adjusting a high crown or replacing missing back teeth.

## When to see someone quickly

If your jaw locks open or shut, if the pain started after a fall or a blow to the face, or if there's swelling, come in the same day.

## Questions

**My jaw clicks but doesn't hurt. Should I worry?**
Usually not. A painless click is common. Come in if it starts to hurt or catch.

**Can a wisdom tooth cause jaw pain near the ear?**
Yes. An infected or impacted lower wisdom tooth can ache right up towards the ear, which is why we check the teeth first.

**Is a night guard uncomfortable?**
It takes a few nights to get used to. A guard made from a scan of your teeth is thin and fits closely, so most people stop noticing it quickly.
