Most parents only start looking for a dentist for their child when something hurts. That's the hardest time to make a good choice, because you're in a hurry and your child is already upset. If you can, pick one before you need one.

Here's what we'd look for.

## Do they talk to your child, or only to you?

A good children's dentist explains things to the child in words they understand: counting teeth, showing the water spray, letting them hold the mirror. If the conversation happens over your child's head, that's a sign.

## Is the first visit allowed to be easy?

The first visit should mostly be about getting comfortable. Sitting in the chair, looking at their teeth on a screen, maybe a quick clean. Unless something is hurting, treatment can wait for another day. A child who leaves the first visit happy is far easier to treat later.

## Can you stay in the room?

For young children, having a parent close by makes a big difference. Ask before you book.

## What do they offer for nervous children?

Some children are anxious whatever you do. Laughing gas (nitrous oxide) is a mild sedative breathed through a small nose mask. The child stays awake and can talk, feels calm, and it wears off within minutes. It's worth knowing whether a clinic has it before you need it.

## Do they try to save milk teeth?

Some people still say milk teeth don't matter because they'll fall out anyway. But a milk tooth lost years early lets the neighbouring teeth drift, and the adult tooth can come in crooked. A dentist who fills, caps or does a root canal on a milk tooth when it makes sense is thinking about your child's adult teeth too.

## Do they tell you what can wait?

Not every small spot needs drilling straight away. A good dentist tells you what needs doing now, what can be watched, and what you can do at home to help.

## Can you get there easily?

Children do better with short, regular visits, so a clinic that's easy to reach and open at weekends helps. Our Rajouri Garden clinic is open all seven days, a short walk from the metro. More on our [children's dentistry page](/kids-dentist-delhi.html).

## Questions

**When should my child first see a dentist?**
By their first birthday, or within six months of the first tooth coming through.

**How often should children have check-ups?**
Every six months for most children, so small problems are caught early.

**What time of day is best?**
Mornings, for most young children, when they're fresh. Avoid nap times if you can.
